# Two-sentence horror candidates (draft pass)

Exactly two sentences each, at most 400 characters total:
the scariest story possible in two moves.

1. The cat: "The scratching inside the walls stopped the
   night we got the cat, and for a week we slept like
   children. Then the vet found the microchip: the cat had
   been dead for two years, and something had been wearing
   it to stay close to us." -- relief, then the relief was
   the trap.
2. The spare key: "We changed the locks after the break-in
   and gave the only spare key to my mother, who lives
   abroad. This morning the spare key was warm, on the
   kitchen table, next to two cups." -- domestic invasion
   arithmetic.
3. The recording: "For my daughter's first steps I filmed
   eleven seconds, and we watched them every year on her
   birthday. In the version from her eighteenth, someone
   behind the camera -- behind me -- starts clapping a
   half-second before I do." -- the archive changing.
4. The tenant: "The landlord said the flat was cheap
   because the previous tenant still received mail here.
   Tonight the mail slot opened from the inside." -- short
   and bodily.
5. The stairs: "Our toddler learned to count the stairs
   aloud: thirteen, every morning, proud as a bell. There
   are twelve, and this morning she counted down." --
   counting horror, very cold.

## Verdict

Winner: candidate 5. Two-sentence horror is a mechanism
contest, and the stairs mechanism is the coldest: a wrong
number (thirteen stairs, twelve exist) installed gently in
sentence one as a child's pride, then a single changed word
in sentence two -- she counted DOWN -- which re-reads the
whole ritual as something descending, practising. The cat
(candidate 1) explains more; the stairs explain nothing,
which is why they stay scary after the tab closes.
